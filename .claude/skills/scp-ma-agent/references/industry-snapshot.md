# Foundry Industry Snapshot — for the SCP CIM Narrative

Use for the market/competition section of the CIM and to frame SCP's end-market
story. Snapshot is early-2026; **refresh the forecast and tariff status live**
when building buyer-facing material (SKILL §5).

## Sizing & structure (NAICS 3315)
- U.S. is **3rd globally** in casting production (behind China, India), **2nd in
  productivity per plant** (behind Germany).
- **~160,000 direct U.S. jobs**, ~300,000 supply-chain jobs; ~1,075 corporate AFS
  members.
- **Outlook (AFS Metalcasting Forecast):** January 2025 projected +4.2% to ~$50.1B;
  **revised down to +1.9%** in the July 2025 mid-year update. Per AFS, 2024 was
  unusually slow and 2025 growth "masks a wide divergence" — some foundries see
  robust demand, others sharp declines. **Confirm the current forecast on refresh.**
- IBISWorld: ferrous foundries are "a **mature, structurally challenged industry**"
  (reduced demand, consolidation, diminished exports) — which is *why* multiples
  sit ~3–4 turns below the broad middle market.

## Tariff backdrop (2025) — a tailwind for domestic foundries
2025 saw sweeping **25% tariffs on imported steel and aluminum**, favoring
domestic casting — a positive for the SCP narrative, but pair it with the
end-market cyclicality caveat.

## End-market positioning (the multiple lever)
Rank SCP's mix and tell the story accordingly:
- **Premium:** aerospace / defense (certified, sticky) — *the single biggest
  multiple lever.*
- **Solid:** infrastructure / waterworks, industrial machinery, energy.
- **Cyclical (discount risk):** automotive, ag, construction, commercial vehicle.
A diversified mix with real aero/defense content is the story that moves the
multiple toward the high end of the foundry range.

## Foundry-specific value drivers (mirror in CIM + readiness)
Capabilities mix (sand/investment/die; alloy range; part-size envelope) ·
end-market diversification (aero = lever) · customer concentration (top-5 <50%) ·
**value-add machining content** (machined > raw) · environmental posture (clean
permits, no NOVs, sand reuse) · capex profile (modern furnaces/molding vs.
deferred) · energy & scrap hedging · workforce (apprenticeship, retention, union
status).
