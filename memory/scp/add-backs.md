## QoE-survival rules (apply to every line)
- **Personal-expense add-backs** survive only with GL-line detail and a clear non-business purpose. Vehicles, travel, memberships are the most-contested.
- **One-time legal** survives only if the matter is closed or limited; recurring "one-times" don't survive.
- **Run-rate adjustments** need signed contracts or customer letters; pipeline doesn't survive.
- **ERC** — confirm IRS posture; some advisors strip it out entirely.
- **Capex** is not an EBITDA add-back — flag growth vs. maintenance separately to frame the multiple, don't bake it into EBITDA.

## Aggressive vs. survivable — the rule of thumb
Build a **defensive** Adjusted EBITDA first (the one the buyer's QoE will agree with), and a **stretched** version with the additional add-backs you'd argue. Show the owner both. The defensive number anchors negotiation; the stretched number anchors the CIM narrative.
